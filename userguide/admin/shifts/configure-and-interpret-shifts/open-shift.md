# use an open shift

## use an open shift

use open shift when employees work without the same fixed daily start and end pattern used by regular schedules and their actual open shift time must be reviewed through the supported workflow.

**Navigation:**\
go to **Shifts → Shifts Planning → Open Shift**.

**Important:** open shift is not a shortcut for missing shift setup. use it only when the approved work arrangement genuinely requires open shift recording and review.

### how is open shift different?

* it does not use the ordinary fixed daily start/end schedule.
* it has separate employee open shift logs and adjustment records.
* open shift time can require review through the configured approval workflow.
* holiday timekeeping can be enabled on the open shift definition.
* the ordinary **Mark as Rest Day** assignment is not supported for an employee whose applicable schedule is open shift.

### how do i create an open shift?

1. go to **Shifts → Shifts Planning → Open Shift**.
2. select **Create New**.
3. enter a clear schedule name.
4. choose the applicable template when required.
5. add notes that explain the intended use.
6. review **Timekeep Normally On Holiday Schedule**.
7. select **Save** to create the schedule.
8. assign it to the intended payroll group or employee dates.

### find or update an open shift definition

The **Open Shift** tab has its own searchable list. Use **Create New** to add a definition, open a row to inspect its details, or download the list as XLS/CSV. Deleting a selected definition is a separate action from changing an employee's dated assignment; review the warning and affected use before confirming a deletion.

To change an existing definition, open its row, select **Edit**, and review the fields shown. Select **Update** and complete its confirmation to change that definition, or **Cancel** to leave it unchanged. **Save as New** is available only while editing a supported non-default definition and creates a separate one. Check the intended employee assignments and open payroll after changing a definition in use.

### verify or correct an open shift setup

confirm the schedule appears under **Open Shift**, assign it to a representative employee or payroll group, and review the resulting employee calendar and supported open shift entry flow. if the wrong definition was saved, correct it or create the intended schedule before broad assignment. after a worked-time correction or approval, recalculate the affected timesheet and open payroll and verify the resulting hours and premiums.

### how does the employee record time?

the employee records actual worked time through the available Open Shift time-entry or adjustment action for their assigned schedule. The entry can then appear in open shift logs and the applicable approval queue. Check the employee's assigned shift and the resulting timesheet before treating the work as complete.

the employee or administrator should provide the correct date, time, and reason required by the form. an open shift definition alone does not create worked hours.

### what should the approver review?

* employee and covered date;
* actual start and end;
* reason or supporting detail;
* overlapping ordinary timesheets or another open shift entry;
* rest-day or holiday status;
* adjustment history; and
* downstream hours and payroll result.

### how do holidays affect open shift?

the open shift setup can allow normal timekeeping on a holiday schedule. holiday identification still comes from the company holiday calendar and the employee's applicable location.

recorded holiday work is not automatically proof of approved premium pay. review the holiday-pay settings, employee entitlement, approval state, and payroll result.

### what should i check after approval or correction?

1. confirm the open shift entry appears under the correct employee and date.
2. confirm the approved or corrected hours.
3. review the holiday or rest-day label when applicable.
4. recalculate the affected timesheet or open payroll.
5. confirm the payslip and reports use the corrected result.
