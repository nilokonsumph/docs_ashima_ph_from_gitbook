# understand your contributions and tax

your employer's contribution settings determine which statutory deductions apply to you, how their calculation basis is built, and when each amount is taken from payroll. **Deductions → Contributions** lets you review your available SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG/HDMF, and voluntary HDMF records. the [payslip](understand-your-payslip.md) shows the employee amount taken for a particular pay date.

## why are contributions different from one payday to another?

a contribution is generally calculated for a month, but its deduction can be scheduled for the first payroll, second payroll, or every payroll. some schedules also support the last payroll of the month. your employer's setup and your employee contribution method determine the timing. a zero or smaller amount on one payslip does not by itself mean the month's contribution is missing; compare the other payslips in that month.

for SSS, Ashima can use the applicable table or a fixed employee amount. the table method first builds a contribution basis from monthly-equivalent basic pay and any pay components the company has chosen to include. the company may include selected earnings and adjustments and may apply selected attendance reductions. Ashima then uses the matching table range. PhilHealth and HDMF have their own calculation methods and settings. the contribution basis is therefore not always identical to the **Basic Pay** line on one payslip.

## if SSS is deducted every payroll, should both amounts be equal?

not always. one setup can split the monthly employee share evenly across semi-monthly payrolls. another can calculate or rebalance the later deduction after checking what was already taken earlier in the month. for example, if the applicable monthly employee share is ₱1,000, a split may show ₱500 and ₱500. a different configured calculation or a changed basis can produce unequal amounts. add the SSS employee deductions across the relevant month and ask payroll to check the saved rule if the total still looks wrong.

Mia's fictional payslips show a ₱500 SSS employee deduction in each semi-monthly run, a PhilHealth deduction only in the first, and an HDMF deduction only in the second. this can be a schedule difference, not missing deductions. the ₱500 SSS figures illustrate one possible split, not Mia's statutory rate. she compares both payslips for the month before raising a question.

## where can I check withholding tax?

open **Deductions → Witholding Tax** when available and check the withholding-tax line on the relevant payslip. Withholding tax is not a statutory contribution. it can change when taxable earnings, applicable exemptions, year-to-date values, or payroll adjustments change. if the amount is surprising, send HR or payroll the period and the taxable-pay and tax lines; they can review the payroll and year-end tax calculation.

{% hint style="info" %}
an employer contribution shown beside your employee contribution is information about the employer's share. do not subtract that share from your net pay unless the payslip includes it in your own **Total Deductions**.
