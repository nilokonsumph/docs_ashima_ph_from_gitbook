# understand your deductions

a deduction reduces the amount paid to you. depending on your employer's setup, it may happen once, recur between two dates, or stop after a set number of payroll occurrences. attendance deductions, contributions, tax, and loans have their own explanations; this article focuses on **Other Deductions** and helps you distinguish them on a payslip.

## where can I check a deduction?

open **Deductions → Other Deductions** or its available history view to see your assigned records. open the [payslip](understand-your-payslip.md) for a pay date to check whether the amount was actually taken in that payroll. a current assignment and an earlier payslip can have different amounts or dates.

## why does a deduction appear in only one payroll?

your employer chooses the pay date or payroll schedule when assigning the item. a one-time deduction is scheduled once. a recurring deduction can be limited by start and end dates or by a number of occurrences. it may therefore appear in the first payroll of a month, the second payroll, or another configured occurrence rather than on every payslip.

for example, Mia has a ₱200 equipment deduction scheduled once and a separate ₱150 approved deduction scheduled for three payroll occurrences. seeing both assignments does not mean ₱350 is taken on every payday. she checks each payslip's deduction lines and the dates of the assignments to find the matching occurrence.

## what if the amount seems too high or continues too long?

check the exact deduction name, amount, pay date, and earlier payslips. make sure you are not counting an item shown in **Total Deductions** again as a second deduction. then ask HR or payroll to review the approved amount, schedule, remaining occurrences, and any change to the assignment. a change to an active assignment does not automatically undo a deduction in a closed payroll.

for SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG/HDMF, and withholding tax, see [contributions and tax](understand-your-contributions-and-tax.md). for loan amortization, see [loans](understand-your-loans.md). for late, undertime, or absence effects, compare your [timesheet](../timesheets-attendance-and-overtime/review-your-timesheet-every-day.md), approved requests, and the payslip.
