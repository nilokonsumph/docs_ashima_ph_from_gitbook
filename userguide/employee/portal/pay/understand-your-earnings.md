# understand your earnings

your basic salary is only one part of your pay. your employer may also assign allowances, De Minimis benefits, and other earnings. some items recur, while others are paid once. the item's setup determines its amount, effective dates, tax classification, and which payroll includes it.

## where can I see an earning?

open **Earnings → Allowances**, **De Minimis**, or **Other Earnings** in Employee portal to review available assignments. use **Other Earnings → History** for earlier records. open the [payslip](understand-your-payslip.md) for the pay date to see what that payroll actually included.

an item shown in your assignments is not necessarily payable in every payroll. for example, a monthly allowance may be set for one payroll in the month, while a one-time award appears only on its scheduled pay date.

## why is an earning smaller than its assigned amount?

the payslip amount can differ from the amount assigned to you because the employer may have set the item to follow worked days or hours, absence rules, an effective date, or a particular payroll schedule. an assignment that starts partway through a cutoff may also produce a partial amount. compare the item's name and dates with your shift, timesheet, approved leave, and payslip for that same period. HR or payroll can confirm which rule was used.

do not subtract an absence from the same earning twice when the payslip already shows a reduced earning or a separate attendance deduction. use the itemized pay detail to see which treatment Ashima applied.

## how do I tell whether an earning is taxable?

the earning's payroll setup classifies it as **Taxable** or **Non Taxable**. your payslip shows taxable and non-taxable pay totals for the processed period. the amount or name alone is not enough to determine the tax treatment: two allowances with similar names can have different settings. ask HR or payroll for the item's classification if your assignment page does not show it.

**Non Taxable** on an allowance or Other Earning does not mean that every peso is exempt for the entire year. Ashima's setup identifies these items with the annual **₱90,000 13th Month and other-benefits ceiling**. the 13th Month and other qualifying benefits share that ceiling; it is not a separate ₱90,000 allowance for each item. when the applicable annual total exceeds the available ceiling, a portion can become taxable. review your payslip and year-end tax record if the split changes later in the year.

{% hint style="info" %}
**De Minimis is different.** Each supported De Minimis benefit has its own non-taxable limit. its amount within that limit is tracked separately from the ₱90,000 ceiling. an amount above the item's limit is an **excess De Minimis** amount, not more De Minimis within the limit. check the payslip or ask payroll how that excess was classified in your result.
{% endhint %}

## what can excess De Minimis mean for me?

suppose a benefit's applicable non-taxable limit is ₱2,500 and your employer assigns ₱2,700 for the same coverage. the ₱200 difference is excess. the limit, coverage period, and treatment depend on the benefit type and applicable rules; do not use this illustrative figure as the limit for every De Minimis item. your payslip shows the amount Ashima processed, and HR or payroll can explain any taxable or other-benefit portion.

## example: several earnings for one employee

Mia, a fictional employee, has several earnings. these amounts are examples, not a standard pay package or a calculation of net pay.

| item                | assigned or approved example | why the payslip can differ                                      |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| transport allowance | ₱1,200 monthly               | its selected payroll date or absence rule can affect the amount |
| De Minimis benefit  | ₱2,500 monthly               | its benefit-specific limit and schedule apply                   |
| one-time award      | ₱1,000 once                  | it appears only on its scheduled pay date                       |
| overtime            | ₱450 approved for one cutoff | only payable approved hours for that cutoff are included        |

Mia opens **Earnings** to check what is assigned, then compares each line with the payslip for the relevant pay date. she does not expect all four items to appear in every payslip.

## an earning is missing or looks wrong

check the pay date, item name, effective dates, and whether it is a one-time or recurring item. if it is an attendance-based item, also check the shift, timesheet, and approval status. send HR or payroll those details and the amount you expected. they can check the company item, your assignment, payroll schedule, and completed calculation.

for the tax distinction between a De Minimis limit and the annual other-benefits ceiling, see the [BIR's explanation of excess De Minimis (question 5)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RMC%20No%2050-2018.pdf). your payslip remains the place to check how Ashima processed your amount.
