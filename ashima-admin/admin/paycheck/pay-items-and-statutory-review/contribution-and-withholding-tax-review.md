# Contribution and withholding-tax review

use the Paycheck contribution and withholding-tax pages to review the rules applied to employees and the deductions recorded by payroll.

**Navigation:**\
go to **Paycheck → Contributions** for SSS, PHIC, HDMF, rules, and Withholding Tax. Use **Settings → Contributions** or **Settings → Taxes** for the governing company options.

## where do the values come from?

contribution results depend on several separate layers:

* company calculation, schedule, and inclusion rules;
* employee entitlement and calculation method;
* employee fixed amounts where the selected method supports them;
* pay-run earnings and dates; and
* prior or year-to-date contribution values where the rule uses them.

do not treat the contribution listing or report as the place to change all of these inputs.

## how do i review employee rules?

open the relevant **SSS Rules**, **PHIC Rules**, **HDMF I Rules**, or **HDMF II Rules** page. The current SSS page shows employee details, calculation method, deduction schedule, included pay families, and contribution to date.

review whether Basic Pay, Overtime, Holiday/Premium Pay, Night Shift Differential, Taxable Other Earnings, and Non-Taxable Other Earnings are included for the employee. The available rule families differ by contribution type.

## how do i review recorded contributions?

open **SSS Contribution**, **PHIC Contribution**, or **HDMF Contribution**, then choose the month and year. The contribution listing can show employee and employer shares, pay-date deduction, totals, and remittance references according to the contribution type.

## how do i review withholding tax?

use the Withholding Tax report and Employee Pay Details to compare the employee's taxable-pay basis, current deduction, adjustments, and year-to-date context. Company tax schedule/rule settings and employee tax setup remain separate sources.

## rules and warnings

* a blank calculation method or displayed `~` requires employee-setup review; it does not prove that the employee is exempt.
* a contribution-to-date value is historical context, not the current-run deduction by itself.
* correct the governing setting or employee record first, then recalculate the open payroll.
* use direct Paycheck ingestion only for an approved correction, not to conceal an incorrect reusable rule.
